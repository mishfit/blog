The Wraith Reach and Nokmetia scripts had an audience in mind, but left the next step vague. A comment could start a conversation, and a website visit could help someone investigate a product. I also wanted interested viewers to be able to choose to hear from me again.

Comparing the plans with Ben Settle's email advice made me look more closely at that invitation.

### Say what the emails contain

“Join my newsletter” tells someone how to subscribe. I need to explain what they would receive.

For creator work, I could write about an audience-finding experiment and what I would change after seeing the response. Nokmetia could offer grooming notes and relevant batch announcements. Those are proposed directions, and each needs useful material and a working signup destination before I put it in a script.

A person may enjoy skating, software and grooming while choosing emails about only one of them. The signup promise needs to make that choice clear, and subsequent emails need to respect it.

### Write from the work already happening

A filming decision or an editing problem can become a blog post. An email can carry one observation from it and point to the relevant next step. That gives me something to write without making another video every time.

Settle's [small-list advice](https://bensettle.com/blog/how-to-profit-from-small-lists/) encourages writing to the list while it is still tiny. I want to build that writing into the work from the beginning, with a specific reason for someone to keep reading.
