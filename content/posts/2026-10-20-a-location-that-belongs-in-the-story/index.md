The whale had become an easy answer to where I should skate for a video. I wanted another answer.

Behind the ballet theater in downtown Salt Lake City is a ballerina mural. It seemed like a good place to practice spins. I would be trying to make the movement look deliberate, with painted examples of grace nearby.

### Give the outing a destination

The mural gives the workout and street skate somewhere to lead. I can establish the destination early and use the arrival to introduce the movement I want to practice.

I want a spin I can repeat with an intentional exit. The first and final attempts need comparable angles and a full view of the movement. Rotating the edited view around me would make the comparison difficult to judge.

The ending has to wait for the session. An improved exit would give me something to explain. An unsuccessful attempt would tell me what to return to next time.

There is also an optional joke about a very quiet coaching staff. A glance at the mural and perhaps one callback should be enough.

Choosing the location settled several decisions before filming. I had a destination and a movement goal, while the result was still open.
